# R60

## Rule

**R60.** The lock-out net: confirm, or it goes back. A change that could lock the owner out runs only with a put-back
armed first, in the Spark's own systemd, which fires with the console, the network or Tailscale down.

- **Armed in the same call, before anything changes.** The root helper's `run --net` (R42; never with `--open`, whose
  groups could reach the timer) refuses while another guard is open on that Spark (`pending`, neither kept nor put back,
  not the proof's own), then, as root, copies the ways in to `/var/lib/spark-console/guard/<id>/`, writes `restore.sh`
  and `guard.py` beside them, its lock, `pending` and the unit `sw-guard-boot-<id>` that runs the same put-back if the
  Spark restarts inside the window (held to 300 s), and arms the timer `sw-guard-<id>` for its time limit plus 600 s. A
  change in it is held to 30 minutes. If arming fails or the call is hung up meanwhile, what this call made is taken
  away and nothing runs; a refusal never touches a guard this call did not make. Copies over 7 days old go when a guard
  is armed, never an open guard's.
- **The ways in**: `/etc/ssh`, `/etc/default/ssh`, `/etc/hosts.allow`, `hosts.deny`, `/etc/nologin`, `/etc/sudoers`,
  `sudoers.d`, `/etc/sudo.conf`, `passwd`, `group`, `shadow`, `gshadow`, `nsswitch.conf`, `/etc/pam.d`, `/etc/security`,
  the system's and the owner's shell start files, the owner's `authorized_keys`, `authorized_keys2` and ssh `config`,
  `/etc/netplan`, `/etc/systemd/network`, NetworkManager's connections, `/etc/hosts`, `/etc/ufw`, `/etc/default/ufw`,
  `/etc/iptables`, `/etc/nftables.conf`, Tailscale's state file and `/etc/default/tailscaled`, systemd's files for ssh
  and tailscaled by name, the owner's linger file and service manager's units, and on the hub the console's sign-in
  files but `sessions.json` (losing sessions is a sign-in). A file is copied with its bytes, mode and owner; a link as a
  link, never followed; a folder with all in it; a path that is not there as not there. Each folder above them, up to
  the owner's home (and `/var/lib/tailscale`, `/var/lib/systemd/linger`), is kept as a folder: its mode and owner. Kept
  as text, to compare: Tailscale's settings and routes, the live firewall rules, and the running state: whether ssh,
  `ssh.socket`, sshd, tailscaled, NetworkManager, networkd and ufw ran and were enabled, ufw on or off, and the key
  Tailscale was signed in with.
- **After the command** it says what differs: what goes back by itself, and when; apart, what does not (the live
  firewall rules; Tailscale's settings when its state file is as it was); and, by name, what cannot be put back at all
  (a Tailscale sign-out or a sign-in as another key, a unit whose package went). It disarms only on the console's word
  (`keep`, after its check from outside), never by itself. If its timer was stopped while the command ran, what differs
  goes back at once, and says so. So does a change under `/etc/ssh` that `sshd -t` refuses, with sshd's words (a refused
  reload would leave every check passing until sshd's next start). A put-back that failed while it ran is said ("net:
  the put-back failed").
- **The put-back** (`restore.sh`: from the timer, after a restart, or `putback`) stops the command's unit, counted
  stopped only when systemd says inactive or failed (no answer is not gone; SIGKILL after a minute; looked at again
  before each try; after a restart, not waited for); one never seen stopped is still put back, and the put-back fails
  (P-1). It puts back only what differs: exact bytes, mode and owner, through folders opened one by one without
  following a link; a folder above that is gone is made again, but only `~/.ssh`, the console's state folder,
  `/var/lib/tailscale` and `/var/lib/systemd/linger` are swapped back for a folder when a link stands there; at the home
  or a folder between a link is left, and what is under it is named not put back. What the change added in a copied
  folder is taken away, and a folder above the copy did not see only once it is empty. Tailscale and the console are
  stopped first and started again on every way out (H4); it tells systemd, restarts `ssh.socket` when it ran (a moved
  port) and reloads what it put back (`stopped` and `put` name both, for a later try). Then each way-in unit whose file
  state moved goes back to enabled, disabled or masked, and one settled at the copy and now (running, or stopped or
  failed) the other way is started or stopped; one still starting or stopping now is waited for, then counts by where it
  is headed; one starting or stopping at the copy is left as it runs and named. After a restart a unit's file is put
  right and one that ran at the copy whose file state differed at the first look (by its links too) is queued to start;
  none is stopped then (A-15). Otherwise one that ran at the copy must be active (one starting: looked at again).
  ufw goes on or off, but not while one of its own files could not be put back. An error on one path
  ends that path only. What is not put back by design (the live rules, Tailscale's settings, what cannot be, a link at
  the home, a unit passing at the copy) is noted; what could not be (a file, a folder made or opened, a unit systemd did
  not say ok to, a reload, ufw or the console's start that failed; not a reload of a unit that is not there) fails it
  (A-14): no `result`; `part` says what went back and what could not; `pending`, the restart unit and the timer stay.
  `result`'s first line counts what went back, earlier tries' too, and what did not. A put-back that fails is tried
  three times, 20 s apart, each failure written down in `failures`. Once kept or put back it only clears what is left
  (`pending`, the restart unit).
- **`keep`** counts only while the timer still waits and once its stop succeeded; after a put-back it answers "already
  put back at …" with the result's count. Once a put-back failed (`failures`), only `keep --as-left` keeps, timer or not
  (stopped first), saying what did not go back; none while the restart's look waits. **`putback`** puts back now, also
  with the timer gone; after a keep it puts nothing back. `check` names an open guard, when it goes back and whether its
  timer waits, then "net look" while the restart's look waits, "net failed" after a failure, or "net left" with no
  timer. A walled command cannot stop or change the timer, write the guard's folder, take `pending` away, or make `keep`
  or `putback` count: the proof tries each (attempts 15 to 18, R42) on two marked guards of its own (they block no
  guard; the next proof takes one left).
- **The console's half** is R71.

## Why

A change to SSH, the firewall, Tailscale or a sign-in can cut off every way the owner reaches a Spark, and the console
with it. Like `netplan try` and a router's "commit confirmed", the change goes back by itself unless it is confirmed,
from a timer nothing a change can reach (the one page, 4 and 7; design A 2.8, 2.13).
