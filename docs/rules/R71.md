# R71

## Rule

**R71.** The lock-out net, the console's half (A13b, `21-net.mjs`; on: `NET_ON`). Moved from R60 on 2026-11-15.

- **Only as root.** On a compute Spark a lock-out change is done only as root (R70): as the owner it changes nothing,
  so it is answered "set root", nothing asked (`netChange`; given `minutes`, it is refused: as root it is held to 10
  minutes and takes none); the seam refuses a non-root run there (`netGuarded`).
- **Only on the owner's words.** As root it is the dangerous ask (R54) only when a sentence of the owner's current
  message tells it to change a way in there (`netAsks`; its own sentences only: a code block, a `>` quote or a
  pasted log holds no ask of theirs, `ownSentences`), with nothing read since (a read that returned, by its record:
  a call refused before it ran is none); never the message before, nor a carry-on turn's (`netNowSaid`). Its line 2
  is then "You asked for this.". Else refused, saying how to ask. What the net can't put back (`lasting`) is not done.
- **The press runs it.** The Spark's claim, the ways in tried (a new SSH connection, never a shared one: `fresh` in
  `02-ssh.mjs`; the helper through sudo; Tailscale online; one down, nothing runs), then its memory cap (root's 8 GB,
  Spark 2's 2 GB) must fit in what is free beyond the 8 GB kept (R2), the guard on record (`net.json`), the journal's
  start line (none written, nothing runs), then `root --net`. The helper's answer is read from the true end of what
  the call printed (`guardCall` keeps its first part and its last 64 KB, and says when the middle was cut); what the
  command printed keeps every line but the helper's own (`netOutput`).
- **Kept or put back.** After it the ways in are tried again from outside: all that worked still work, `keep`; one
  doesn't, `putback` at once; no answer, the Spark's own timer, claimed until then. `keep` only after the helper's "net
  open": a put-back it tried at once that failed, or an answer this build doesn't know, is never kept but left to the
  timer, claimed, and said. A put-back the helper made at once is said with its reason: sshd's own words (the Spark's
  output, masked, marked as data) or its timer gone (`netWhyBack`). A put-back is read as one of three (`guardAnswer`):
  whole ("put back at …: N went back, M did not", the M by design); in part (the helper's count when it did not
  finish, "N went back, M could not: <the first>", in putback's refusal or the net's line at once: `GUARD_COUNT`),
  told with what went back and the first of what could not, in the helper's words, never as put back nor blamed on
  live state (`netPartWords`); or failed with no count (nothing of it is known to have gone back), never told as a
  Spark the put-back couldn't reach. After either the guard stays open, on the helper and on record (`unfinished`,
  with the count), whatever its time, until a put-back finishes or the owner keeps it; and every word about it names
  both ways on (`netWaysOn`): `/putback` tries again, or the owner keeps it as it stands with one command, run on that
  Spark in their own terminal, exactly as typed with the guard's id (`sudo /usr/local/sbin/spark-console-root keep
  --id <id>`), which says what did not go back; after it `/putback` finds it kept. A second change there is refused
  with both (`netOpenWords`; on Spark 2 too, before its ask), and so says the needs-you line (`netNeeds`). The press
  reads the guard's record again before it marks it open and before `keep`: one a `/putback` reached while it ran (its
  mark, or what it wrote) is never kept, and what `/putback` found is said (put back while it ran, the helper's "net:
  already put back"; not finished, with both ways). A call the helper refuses after arming (its unit never started:
  nothing ran) has its guard taken away at once by `putback`, so the Spark is free for the next change. A `putback`
  call has 660 s, past the helper's worst case with one hung systemd call a try, not with more: then it reads as no
  answer (`GUARD_PUTBACK_S`, its count beside it). Each outcome is told once.
- **What is said to go back.** The ask's net line (`netFact`) and the words after a put-back (`netStillWords`,
  `netRestart`) say exactly this: the ways-in files and units the net copied go back; live firewall and network state
  does not (a restart of the Spark clears it); nothing else the change did goes back, and a restart doesn't undo it.
- **After.** Sparkie and agents never reach `keep` or `putback`; `/stop` never stops the timer; `/undo` after a change
  made through the net puts nothing back and points to `/putback` (R34); `/putback` (page or paired chat) tries every
  open guard, oldest first, one answer each (one alone is "the newest"; with none open it names the newest kept one,
  which stays; one kept as it stands is said so; one kept, or no longer on the helper (off the open list), lets its
  Spark go). While one is open there, a second is refused unasked. At start each open guard on record
  is put back (or found back or kept), said; one its Spark doesn't answer for keeps that Spark claimed until its time,
  then is looked at again; one whose put-back did not finish, or turns so then, is not tried again, only looked at once
  its time comes. After its time and at start, a put-back the helper says failed is said as that (the Spark answers;
  the change may still be in place; both ways on; where the failures are written), never as a Spark that doesn't
  answer; one not finished whose Spark is dark is said as not finished (what did not go back may cut it off; a restart
  makes it try again; both ways); no helper there, or another build's, is said so with the owner's command; a keep
  found then is told. The record cuts only closed guards past the newest 50.
- **Folders kept read-only** (`--protect`, Spark 2's change): one that is a link is passed as the folder it leads to;
  one that leads to no folder stops the change, never runs it unprotected (`protectShell`, `21-guard.mjs`).

## Why

R60's Why: a change to SSH, the firewall, Tailscale or a sign-in can cut off every way the owner reaches a Spark. The
console's half decides who may ask for such a change, runs it only after the owner's press, and tells the owner what
became of it.

## Today

As R60's Today. What the owner's words ask is word lists (`netAsks`): the way-in word must be what the change verb
acts on, in the six words after it, before a comma, a joining word or a place word (on, in, of, from, at), and not
the start of another thing's name ("the shell script", `NET_NAMED`). A miss is refused, never run. Each change word is also put to the intent
sorter's order rule (`netOrderAt` calls `orderAt`, 16-actions.mjs, A-18): it is an order only as the sentence's first
word or after a short closed list of openers ("as root on Spark 3,", "please", "can you"). Narration ("I used to
restart tailscale on Spark 3 every week."), reported speech ("My colleague says to turn the firewall off on Spark 3.")
and an order after a statement in one sentence ("I can't log in to Spark 3, reset my password") are refused, saying
an order starts its sentence (`NET_NOT_ORDER`); where it starts is the intent builder's rule (F7, F8 wait on it).
"Kill", "end" or "terminate" a process or pid is a way-in change: as root a kill by number is the lock-out class. Keeping a change whose put-back did not finish
is the owner's one command on that Spark: a `/keep` word is not built (contract 8.13's list of the owner's words is the
owner's, and a lock-out risk is never an action); it waits for the owner. Lines moved: `docs/history/later-2026-11-15.md`.
