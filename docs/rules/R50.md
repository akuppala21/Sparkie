# R50

## Rule

**R50.** Any brain (contract 3.11; design B, step 9): the chat loop keeps no model's habits. How a brain is asked and
read is its setup's: `thinking`, `maxTokens`, `sampling` and `templateKwargs`, as today's setups have them, and
`mergeRoles`, `thinkTags` and `tokenBudget`. A setup may put these in an `ask` block, whose fields go over the same
names beside it (`withAsk`). A Library model's `spark.json` may carry an `ask` block, and the setup made from it keeps
it, checked (`askBlock`): a field that fails its check is left out, and the limits (`toolRounds`, `maxChanges`,
`toolCap`, `workSteps`, `contextTokens`) are no ask, so a block never sets them. The chat loop (`howAsked`,
`brainContext`, `modelStep`, `turnRounds`) and the brain check (`brainAsk`) go through `13-ask.mjs`. With `mergeRoles`,
two messages of one role in a row are sent as one (`askedMessages`); a tool call or its result never is. Thinking is
read from `reasoning_content` or `reasoning`, and a thinking block the server left at the start of the text, between the
`thinkTags` (`<think>` and `</think>` unless the setup says otherwise), is held back and dropped: never shown, saved or
sent back (`answerReader`). With a `tokenBudget` every request asks for the brain's own count of tokens
(`stream_options`), and once the last answer's `usage` (what it read and wrote) reaches the budget, the next round
offers no tools, as after the last round (`overBudget`). The loop's own budgets end a turn the same way, each counted by
code per message (`turnBudgets`): work steps, the `run` and `write_file` calls (`workSteps`, else twice `toolRounds`,
at most 30 a message, and 120 a task over all its turns, `taskStepsKept`; past them a call is not run), tool output (40% of the setup's context at 3
characters a token: `contextTokens`, else the number after the last of its `--context-length`, `--max-model-len` or
`--ctx-size`, as an engine reads them, else 32,768, `budgetContext`; `memorySizes` reads the same flags, `contextFlag`
in `20-memory-names.mjs`; a result past it, and each after it, is a notice naming its journal record, `budgetCut`), and
three refused calls in a row (an error, or a `run` that ran nothing; one that works resets it). A round runs at most
4 calls; each past them is answered "not run" (on the page, in the journal, to the brain and in the report). In a round
the lookups run together, and every call that may change something (a change, `run`, `write_file`) after them, one at a
time in the order asked, each once the one before it ended (up to 2 minutes past its 50 seconds, `roundCalls`); one
refused because an operation of the same turn still holds its Spark is no refusal. `toolRounds` is held to
40 and `maxChanges` to 12 (`setupLimits`). Which limits a turn gets is its brain's profile's (`turnLimits`, R52): the
setup's own once its brain is checked, else at most 8 rounds and 16 work steps a message. The rounds and the token
budget are budgets too. The last request of a turn a budget ended offers no tools and tells the brain so (`toolsAway`);
a call it makes all the same is not run (`roundDropped`). The answer then ends with code's report: what ran (one asked
first is counted apart, as waiting for the owner's yes), what was not read, what was refused and why, that the rounds or
the tokens were used up, and the calls not run. After the steps, the output, the rounds or the tokens, the owner's
request that is a task (a carry-on turn's, or one with a next step) carries on in a fresh turn within the carry-on's own
limits (`budgetCarries`, `continueTask`), and one its own limit stops there gets the stop mark (`keepTask`, R66);
otherwise, after a task's 120, and always after three refusals, the owner says
"go on", which starts with fresh budgets (a task stopped at a limit keeps its request: R66). Three refusals end the
carrying on even in the round whose command reached the steps. (The long task itself: R66.) A
setup that sets none of `mergeRoles`, `thinkTags` and `tokenBudget` is sent byte for byte what it was before, so the
tested mark (`brainReads`) is unchanged; one that sets them changes the mark (`brainCatalog`) and its brain profile's
key (`brainSetupOf`). So do its limits, `workSteps` and `contextTokens` among them (`brainLimits`).

## Why

The owner will swap in newer brains (decisions 8 and 14): a model with a stricter chat template, another field for its
thinking or a smaller context must work by its setup, with no edit to the chat loop.

## Today

- The defaults are the first brain's habits, so a new brain's setup has to say what it needs. Nothing finds it out by
  itself: the brain check does not probe message order, tags or usage.
- Thinking is switched through `chat_template_kwargs` (`enable_thinking`), always sent; `templateKwargs` adds keys to it.
- A thinking block is found only at the very start of an answer. A template that writes the opening tag itself (the
  answer starts inside the thinking and only the closing tag comes) is not handled: such a brain needs a reasoning
  parser on its server. An answer that really starts with `<think>` is taken as thinking.
- The token budget counts from the first answer on (a request's size is known only once it is answered), and bounds only
  the rounds: history and tool results are still cut by characters.
- Outside the chat loop the first brain's habits stay: `answerTest` and `answers` (`09-verified.mjs`, in the start
  workflows' version) and `searchTerms` (`10-recipes.mjs`) send `enable_thinking: false` and read `reasoning_content`.
- The loop's budgets count characters, not tokens, and no setup states `workSteps` or `contextTokens` yet: from their
  flags, aphex has 12 commands a message and 1,200,000 characters, the agent setups 30 and 314,572. llama.cpp's short
  `-c` is not read (32,768). A plain question that reaches a budget waits for "go on"; that message starts from nothing.
- A brain not checked keeps its setup's changes a message, calls a round (4) and tool output: only its rounds and work
  steps are cut (design E 2.3 asks 1 change, 1 call a round and half the output; each change is a button then anyway).
- A task's 120 is counted on its task record (`steps`), in work steps handed over, whether or not they ran.
- Merged messages are joined by a blank line, so with no record between them a notice's text runs straight into the
  owner's next words.
