# R50

## Rule

**R50.** Any brain (contract 3.11; design B, step 9): the chat loop keeps no model's habits. How a brain is asked and
read is its setup's: `thinking`, `maxTokens`, `sampling` and `templateKwargs`, as today's setups have them, and
`mergeRoles`, `thinkTags` and `tokenBudget`. A setup may put these in an `ask` block, whose fields go over the same names
beside it (`withAsk`). A Library model's `spark.json` may carry an `ask` block, and the setup made from it keeps it,
checked (`askBlock`): a field that fails its check is left out, and the limits (`toolRounds`, `maxChanges`, `toolCap`,
`workSteps`, `contextTokens`) are no ask, so a block never sets them. The chat loop (`howAsked`, `brainContext`, `modelStep`, `turnRounds`) and the
brain check (`brainAsk`) go through `13-ask.mjs`. With `mergeRoles`, two messages of one role in a row are sent as one
(`askedMessages`); a tool call or its result never is. Thinking is read from `reasoning_content` or `reasoning`, and a
thinking block the server left at the start of the text, between the `thinkTags` (`<think>` and `</think>` unless the
setup says otherwise), is held back and dropped: never shown, saved or sent back (`answerReader`). With a `tokenBudget`
every request asks for the brain's own count of tokens (`stream_options`), and once the last answer's `usage` (what it
read and wrote) reaches the budget, the next round offers no tools, as after the last round (`overBudget`). The loop's
own budgets end a turn the same way, each counted by code per message (`turnBudgets`): `run` calls (`workSteps`, else
twice `toolRounds`, at most 30; past them a call is not run), tool output (40% of the setup's context at 3 characters
a token: `contextTokens`, else the number after its `--context-length`, `--max-model-len` or `--ctx-size`, else 32,768,
`budgetContext`; a result past it, and each after it, is a notice naming its journal record, `budgetCut`), and three
refused calls in a row (an error, or a `run` that ran nothing; one that works resets it). The answer then ends with
code's report: what ran, what was not read, or what was refused and why. After the steps or the output, the owner's
request that is a task (a carry-on turn's, or one with a next step) carries on in a fresh turn within the carry-on's
own limits (`budgetCarries`, `continueTask`); otherwise, and always after three refusals, the owner says "go on". A task
keeps the guards of the turns that made it, and each of its carry-on turns starts with them (`keepTask`, `turnRequest`):
"don't stop anything" said with or just before its request (`noStops`), and that a reply read outside text
(`readOutside`). A pressed No ends it, and so do Disconnect, a new bot token or pairing (a task from Telegram) and
signing out its device (`endTask`, `endTasksFrom`); the end is saved at once. Its limits come from the owner's own
instruction when it is made (`taskBudget`, design E 2.1): 2 hours and 8 carry-on turns; sent off to work on its own ("on
your own", "overnight", "while I'm away", …) 4 hours and 60; asked to work for 1 to 72 hours, those and 15 turns an
hour. Only an instruction counts (a clause opening with a verb of working, no "not" before the words), never quoted,
backticked or pasted text. Every task stops at 120 work steps. At any limit no new turn starts (`taskOver`). A carry-on
turn that ends owing nothing (nothing running, asked or still to end) ends its task. Limits, guards and its way in (its
Telegram pairing too) are kept in `chats.json`: after a restart a task that was on disk carries on
(`tasksAfterRestart`), one whose way in is gone ends, a record from before these limits is dropped, and one waiting on
an operation gets a line that the restart may have cut it off. A state put back ends every task, with a line
(`putBackAtStart`). A job's end wakes its task as a result does. A limit reached marks the reply that reached it, or is
said in a line (`taskStopped`), as is a full chat; it stands as one needs-you line until the owner writes there, and
"carry on" or "go on" gives a fresh budget of the same size (`taskAgain`). A carry-on turn that can't start, or that the
brain never answers, is not counted and is tried again while its hours last (`taskRetry`). On Telegram a task's reply
goes out only when nothing of it is left running or owed, past the hourly limit. Three refusals end the carrying on even
in the round whose command reached the steps.
A setup that
sets none of the three is sent byte for byte what it was before, so the tested mark (`brainReads`) is unchanged; one
that sets them changes the mark (`brainCatalog`) and its brain profile's key (`brainSetupOf`).

## Why

The owner will swap in newer brains (decisions 8 and 14): a model with a stricter chat template, another field for its
thinking or a smaller context must work by its setup, with no edit to the chat loop.

## Today

- The defaults are the first brain's habits, so a new brain's setup has to say what it needs. Nothing finds it out by
  itself: the brain check does not probe message order, tags or usage.
- Thinking is switched through `chat_template_kwargs` (`enable_thinking`), always sent; `templateKwargs` adds keys to it.
- A thinking block is found only at the very start of an answer. A template that writes the opening tag itself (the
  answer starts inside the thinking and only the closing tag comes) is not handled: such a brain needs a reasoning
  parser on its server. An answer that really starts with `<think>` is taken as thinking.
- The token budget counts from the first answer on (a request's size is known only once it is answered), and bounds only
  the rounds: history and tool results are still cut by characters.
- Outside the chat loop the first brain's habits stay: `answerTest` and `answers` (`09-verified.mjs`, in the start
  workflows' version) and `searchTerms` (`10-recipes.mjs`) send `enable_thinking: false` and read `reasoning_content`.
- The loop's budgets count characters, not tokens, and no setup states `workSteps` or `contextTokens` yet: from their
  flags, aphex has 12 commands a message and 1,200,000 characters, the agent setups 30 and 314,572. llama.cpp's short
  `-c` is not read (32,768). A plain question that reaches a budget waits for "go on"; that message starts from nothing.
- Merged messages are joined by a blank line, so with no record between them a notice's text runs straight into the
  owner's next words.
