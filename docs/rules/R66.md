# R66

## Rule

**R66.** Long tasks (design E 2.1 as cut, step 6; contract 4.8): a task carries on by itself only within limits the
owner's own words set, and stops, waits or ends where the owner would expect. A task is the owner's request that carries
on in fresh turns (R50: a carry-on turn's, or one with a next step). A task keeps the guards of the turns that made it,
and each of its carry-on turns starts with them (`keepTask`, `turnRequest`): "don't stop anything" said with or just
before its request (`noStops`), and that a reply read outside text (`readOutside`). A pressed No ends it, and so do
Disconnect, a new bot token or pairing (a task from Telegram) and signing out its device (`endTask`, `endTasksFrom`);
the end is saved at once. Its limits come from the owner's own instruction when it is made (`taskBudget`, design E 2.1):
2 hours and 8 carry-on turns; sent off to work on its own ("on your own", "overnight", "while I'm away", …) 4 hours and
60; asked to work for 1 to 72 hours, those and 15 turns an hour. Only an instruction counts: a clause opening with a
verb of working that its words of time end ("work on it overnight", "for the next 3 hours"), with no "not" before them
and no "what", "of", "from" or the like making them what to look at; never a question, or quoted, backticked or pasted
text. Every task stops at 120 work steps, counted once, on the task (`taskStepsKept`, `taskStepsLeft`, R50). At any
limit no new turn starts (`taskOver`). A carry-on turn that ends owing nothing (nothing running, parked or still to end,
and no result or job's end posted while it answered) ends its task. Limits, guards and its way in (its Telegram pairing
too) are kept in `chats.json`: after a restart a task that was on disk carries on (`tasksAfterRestart`), one whose way
in is gone ends, a record from before these limits is dropped, and one waiting on an operation gets a line that the
restart may have cut it off. A state put back ends every task, with a line (`putBackAtStart`). A job's end wakes its
task as a result does. A limit reached marks the reply that reached it, or is said in a line (`taskStopped`), as is a
full chat; it stands as one needs-you line until the owner writes there, and "carry on" or "go on" gives a fresh budget
of the same size (`taskAgain`). A carry-on turn that can't start, or that the brain never answers, is not counted and is
tried again while its hours last (`taskRetry`). On Telegram a task's reply goes out only when nothing of it is left
running or owed, past the hourly limit. A reply that offered a button, or a request with a next step whose first change
is one, parks its task (contract 4.8, `taskPark`): no turn by itself, its hours stop, one needs-you line; a press of its
own button that runs it wakes the task (`taskAnswered`), never an agent's press nor another request's button; "No" ends
it, "carry on" asks again.

When a task ends (done, at a limit, the owner's `/stop` or a new request there, a No at its question, its way in gone,
a state put back), code writes one report (E7; design E 2.8 as cut, no `report` action), never from the brain's words.
Every journal line of a request carries its id (`task`: the first turn's id, kept as the task's `id`): its turns, their
tool calls, the changes made for it (`ctx.task`) and its operations' end lines (`job.task`). The report counts those
lines (`taskCounts`): operations finished, failed and cancelled; changes at once and after a yes; questions asked; what
was undone (an undo, a cancel); time and turns; watt-hours summed from the end lines, an operation with no number "not
measured", never 0; and what is still open (`taskOpen`). Text others can write is masked and bounded. It is owed on
disk first (`conv.reports`), written once in the task's chat, and told once on Telegram (marked before the send) when
the task began there, asked there at a button, or ran over an hour; at start-up what is owed is finished. A task
stopped at a limit is reported then, not again when left. `/status` and the page's status show a task under way with
its counts so far (`tasksNow`), the page's without its words.

## Why

Work that outlasts one message must not run on past what the owner asked for, come back by itself once it is done or
ended, or stall with no word (the read before landing E6, 2026-11-15).

## Today

- A task's 120 commands count `run` calls only: a task of other lookups is bounded by its turns and hours alone.
- Disconnecting Telegram or signing the device out ends the task, but a carry-on turn already under way finishes its
  rounds (`endTask` does not cancel it as `/stop` does).
- `/pause` holds a task back; it does not end it. After a resume a result, a job's end or a restart wakes it while its
  hours last.
- A dangerous ask (R54) does not park a task: its question is not among the turn's buttons (`taskAsked`).
- "Carry on" typed while a task is neither stopped nor parked is a new request, and ends it.
- A parked task's needs-you line says "Press a button there" also after the button's 15 minutes, and after a restart.
- A reply can end "Carrying on with your request in a fresh turn" and then park, when it also offered a button.
