# R74

## Rule

**R74.** Which of the owner's own words ask for a change (`changeIntent`, R25's first half; C-1, C-6, S-3, A-18; the
final read of 2026-11-16: W1, W2, Q1, C1, R1, and the net's F7, F8), and where they put it (`sparkPlaces`, A-20).

- **Their own sentences only** (`ownSentences`, C-1): code blocks (fenced or indented), `>` lines, quotes or backticks
  of more than three words (a name or path stays: rename "old.txt" to "new.txt"), lines that read as a log, a diff or a
  program's output (and the plain lines between two), and the lines after one ending in ":" up to a blank line are no
  ask. After a note's own heading ("Note to self;", "todo list", "reminder:", `ORDER_NOTE`) the rest of the message is
  the note. The owner's slash words (/stop, /undo …) are answered by code, never asked of Sparkie.
- **A call-off wins** (`calledOff`, C-6, C1): a later sentence that only calls off ("Actually don't.", "Never mind.",
  "Wait.", "Cancel that.", "Not yet.", "nvm", "nah", "no", "nope", "jk", "dont", "changed my mind", "ignore that",
  "scrap that", "disregard") rules out every kind asked before it in the message; one that ends in a call-off rules out
  its own: after a comma, a dash or "…" any of them, after a space only one that can mean nothing else (`OFF_SURE`:
  "stop gemma nvm", "… jk dont", "… actually dont"). For every kind the reader knows.
- **A question asks nothing** but research (Q1): one that opens as a question, or ends in "?" unless "can you" (could,
  would, will) asked it, or ends in "or not", "yes or no", "or what", ", right", "good idea", "which is better".
- **Where an order puts its verb** (`orderAt`, A-18, S-3): a change word of any kind (start, stop, restart, keep,
  power, work, file, memory and the rest) asks only as the first word the owner says to Sparkie in that sentence, after
  nothing but a closed list of openers: marks ("-", "*", "(", "#1", "1)", "/", "…", an emoji); a greeting or a joining
  word (ok, k, kk, so, and, now, then, also, just, yes, thanks, thx, ty, btw, oh, no, yo, quick, morning, gm, hey there,
  wait, do it, please do, Sparkie …: `ORDER_LEAD`); a place or a manner ("on Spark 3", "on 3", "Spark 3:", "Spark 3
  start …", "for Spark 3,", "in Spark 3's firewall,", "as root", "with root on Spark 3,", "use root to", "gemma on 4,",
  "pip install": `ORDER_WHERE`); or a request to Sparkie ("can you", "I want you to", "want you to", "I'd like you to",
  "go ahead (and)", "go (and)", "let's", "do me a favor and", "you can", "you should", "we should", "we need to", "need
  to", "gotta", "time to", "try to": `ORDER_YOU`). "I want to …", "wanna …" are a wish: a button. Whether, if, should,
  can before it are read without a request's own words ("thanks, can you stop gemma" asks).
- **A later part** counts only when the sentence began as an order: a plain command verb (`ORDER_OPENERS`; never a talk
  verb: remember, note, wait, ask, let, go, get, give, keep, watch, have, do, see, tell), or a verb that looks first
  (read, check, look at, find, list, show, get) with nothing before the next part that tells of someone or something (a
  subject, how, why, whether, would, said, used, tried: `ORDER_TOLD_OF`). It comes after a comma, "and", "then", "&",
  "+", "after that", a list's "2)", the earlier order's place ("stop qwen on 3 start gemma on 4"), or a command verb and
  one or two words ("stop qwen start gemma").
- **Never a noun or a report** (W1): ":" or "#" after it, "as", "of", "nothing", "-up", ".sh", "/", "'s"; a finite verb
  or a state right after it ("Build fails", "build is green", "update's stuck", "fix didnt work", "build still
  failing": `ORDER_REPORT`); a state ("broken", "stuck", "done", "required", a word in "-ed") when nothing, a stop mark,
  a small word or a time follows ("build broken on Spark 3"; "fix broken links" is an order: `ORDER_STATE`); an
  adjective before a noun with its own verb ("open ports on Spark 3 are a risk": `ORDER_ADJ`); for a work word also "log",
  "mode", "error", a number, "for", "in the". **Never told of** (F7): a past time or a someday in its clause
  ("yesterday", "last week", "earlier", "ago", "eventually", "at some point", "before" at the end, "already" before
  "and" or "but"), or the change's outcome joined on ("and it still fails", ", still asks …", "but it doesn't work").
  An order has no subject of its own: a habit, someone else's words, a plan or a wish asks for nothing.
- **Where** (A-20, `sparkPlaces`): this message only. A place is a Spark after on, onto, to, into, at, in, across, over,
  for, or first ("Spark 4: start …"); ruled out by not, n't, never, without, avoid (up to three words before it), but
  (right before), except, instead of, rather than, other than, besides, and for a start off and from. Spark spellings:
  "spark four", "s4", "sp4", "spark #4", "spark number 4", "the fourth spark", "sprak 4", "akspark4". Doubtful: "3 or
  4", "the other spark", "both sparks", "node 4", "the 4th one", "on #4", "there" at the end. R25 says what each does.
- **The trade:** a statement and an order in one sentence ("The loader on Spark 3 is broken, fix it.") is a button;
  said as two sentences, it runs at once.
- `orderAt(sentence, match, kind)` is the one function for the order's place; `netAsks` (21-net.mjs) calls it as
  `orderAt(sentence, at)`, with its own verb's index.

## Why

A sentence about a change is not an order to make it. A word missing from the openers costs a button, not a change.

## Today

The reader is a second gate behind the brain's own call: it decides only whether a call that fits the words runs at
once or is a button. No word list is complete: a phrasing it lacks costs a button, and a sentence it misreads can let a
matching call run at once. Measured on the final read's lists (2026-11-16): of 246 plain orders 224 run at once (161
before this read, 211 on 7c6d682); 4 that ran on 7c6d682 are a button ("I want to …", "i wanna …", "wanna …": a wish;
"todo: …": a note). Of 249 that are no order 202 ask nothing (118 before, 53 on 7c6d682): none in the question,
negation, plan or habit, someone else's words, to someone else, note or work-noun groups; still asking: 5 orders with a
condition after ("stop it if it is idle"), 16 pasted lines with no marks, 13 sayings and titles, 11 one-word replies, 2
"remember …" lines. A-18's lists: 0 of 73 ask, 46 of 46 at once, 2 of 8 at once ("we need to", "time to": F8). The net's:
58 of its 71 order sentences are orders at its verb (not a condition, "when you're done,", "I think we should", "have
Spark 3 …", "tell Spark 3 to", a gerund or participle, a command after a colon, nor the four statement-then-order);
2 of its 50 that are no order pass here and its own checks refuse ("…? no way", "read the README … and open the port it
says").
